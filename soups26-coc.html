Code of Conduct Adapted from the USENIX Code of Conduct that was approved by the USENIX Board of Directors on January 22, 2025 We are committed to providing a safe and enjoyable experience for all participants in our communities by offering an environment that welcomes and encourages the free discussion of ideas. We do not tolerate harassment of participants in any form during events, in any online space, or on social media. All event attendees, speakers, sponsors, exhibitors, staff, volunteers, and community participants are required to refrain from harassment and to attempt to avoid potentially offensive imagery or language when participating in SOUPS’26. Our staff will enforce this Code at all times, and expect cooperation from all participants. How to contact the staff from the local organizing team If you have any concerns about inappropriate behavior by any participant during SOUPS’26, please contact the local organizing team: At the event itself, staff can be identified by their clearly marked "Staff" T-Shirts. You can also email staff members directly soups2026-coc@teamusec.de. Harassment Harassment includes—but is not limited to—unwelcome conduct or offensive verbal comments related to protected classes under law; deliberate intimidation, stalking, following, unwelcome or unauthorized photography or recording (including screenshotting or otherwise recording an individual’s participation in virtual events and online community spaces), sustained disruption of talks, other events, or online community spaces, inappropriate physical contact, unwelcome sexual attention, and sexualized images in public spaces. Similarly, encouraging others to engage in such behavior is not permitted, nor are false accusations of harassment. Offensive Imagery or Language We expect that all participants attempt to avoid potentially offensive imagery or language. Regarding images, participants should avoid displaying violent, degrading, or sexualized images, or otherwise create a sexualized environment, including via clothing. Regarding language, participants should especially avoid words where one meaning may be used to refer to another person or group of people in a derogatory manner, e.g. master/slave, blacklist/whitelist, militarized metaphors. For more information on the reasoning behind this request, please see IETF Draft knodel-terminology. Also, since our community is broad and international, we recommend avoiding colloquialisms and culturally specific humor. Authors and presenters are responsible for the content of their work, but we request that they be cognizant of potentially offensive actions, language, or imagery, and that they consider whether it is necessary to convey their message. What should I do if I observe behavior that violates the Code of Conduct? Interpersonal Behavior: If it is safe to do so, we encourage you to ask the person to stop the unwelcome or harassing behavior. Participants who are asked to stop any such behavior are expected to comply immediately. If you are being harassed, notice that someone else is being harassed, or have any related concerns, please contact the local organizing team staff immediately. Sponsor/Exhibitor Complaints: Please contact the local organizing team. Talks and Presentations: We welcome all kinds of feedback on talks and presentations. Attendee comments are taken seriously when planning future events and will be kept confidential upon request. Online Spaces: Our moderators and editors may participate in online spaces (e.g., content moderation during the Q&A session via Slido) and will take appropriate action if they observe harassment or offensive language or imagery. If you observe inappropriate behavior, please contact the staff from the local organizing team. What happens once a report is made? Interpersonal Behavior: If an incident of harassment is reported, our staff will conduct an investigation. If they determine—to the best of their ability, and in their sole discretion—that a participant has violated this Code, they may take any action they deem appropriate, including warning the offender or expulsion from the event (or the online space) with no refund. Under certain circumstances and at their discretion, our staff may contact appropriate law enforcement authorities. To protect all parties involved, we will generally not make any detailed public statements about Code of Conduct incidents. Sponsor/Exhibitor Complaints: Our staff will investigate the complaint. If they determine—to the best of their ability, and in their sole discretion—that action is warranted, they may resolve the matter as they deem appropriate, including removing the exhibit. Talks and Presentations: Concerns will be relayed to current and future organizers, who will take the information into account when selecting speakers for future events. We will strive to be reasonable, with the understanding that determining whether material is "offensive" is inherently subjective. Online Spaces: Because Slido questions are anonymous, we can't follow up directly with the person who submitted a report. Instead, our moderators review all questions and comments before they go live, and anything we consider inappropriate will simply not be published.
